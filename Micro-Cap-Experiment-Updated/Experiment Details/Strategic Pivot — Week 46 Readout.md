## Executive Summary

The Week 41 readout (June 26) declared the pivot's target hit: the portfolio had overtaken its S&P-equivalent by **+$3.09 (+0.4%)** and injection-neutral alpha stood at **+6.34%**. It also carried an explicit warning — *"the alpha is real but not yet durable… the +0.4% surplus is thin enough to flip back quickly."* **Five weeks later, that warning is the story.** As of Week 46 the benchmark gap is back to **−$19.50 (−2.6%)** and TWR alpha has compressed to **+2.87%**. The lead was taken, lost, briefly re-taken (+0.9% on July 29), and given back again. The pivot's *repair* of the original −21.1% deficit has held impressively; the *lead* itself has not. This readout is the honest interim: what round-tripped, why, and what it says about the final seven weeks.

## 4. What Round-Tripped — and the Cost of Two Bad Entries

Since Week 41 the book's equity is actually **down $8.97** ($741.20 → $732.23) on zero injections. Two forces explain almost all of it:

1. **The low-beta lag during the tech rebound (early July).** The exact reversal of the Week 41 tailwind: when mega-cap tech leadership resumed, a small-cap/value/zero-tech book lagged a rising S&P. Caveat #1 of the last readout, realized on schedule.

2. **Two failed new initiations cost ~$9.30 — nearly the entire decline.**
   - **PHAT** (Week 44): a hot momentum entry stopped out −8.8% on day one (−$5.55).
   - **LXU** (Week 46): a nitrogen cyclical entered on a *stale* thesis (an April earnings-call line about "sustained pricing" while fertilizer prices had already fallen five straight weeks); exited −6.1% (−$3.75).

The core winners held; the *new* money lost. Both failures were entry-quality errors, and both produced permanent rule changes — the **Day-1 Drawdown Rule**, **Thesis-Input Freshness** (verify a driver's current level, not an old call), and the **price-data-integrity** rules (never trust an undated quote). The direct cost was small; the process value is that this specific class of loss should not recur.

## 5. Honest Caveats — Skill vs. Tailwind

1. **The lead is not held, and that is the whole point now.** Twice positive, twice given back. Against an S&P making new highs, bursts of catalyst alpha have not compounded into a durable surplus. Calling this a "win" would repeat the Week 41 over-confidence the market just punished.

2. **TWR alpha is *lower* than five weeks ago** (+2.87% vs +6.34%). On the cleanest, injection-neutral measure, the strategy has given back ground since late June — the July peak (+6.87%, Jul 29) only marginally exceeded the June peak before the ATRC pullback erased most of it.

3. **Beta has fallen (1.63 from 1.79) — a mixed signal.** The book is less violent, which cushions down days (see Jul 29), but also means it captures less of a strong up-tape — structurally, that caps how fast a lead can be built when the S&P is trending up.

4. **Concentration risk is real and current.** ATRC's two-day −12% slide (a UBS PT trim, not a downgrade) moved the whole scoreboard from +0.9% to −2.6%. A five-name book lets a single position swing the alpha; that is the price of running concentrated.

## 8. Bottom Line

The Week 30 pivot asked whether diversified, thesis-driven positions could erase a 21-point benchmark deficit. Sixteen weeks on, the answer remains **yes — the deficit is gone** (−21.1% → −2.6%, no new drawdown, zero binary bets). But the Week 41 readout's own caveat has been the last five weeks' lesson: **a thin, regime-assisted lead does not hold itself.** The book took the lead twice and surrendered it twice, its two new entries both failed, and injection-neutral alpha sits below where it was in June. The encouraging part is that the July alpha was *earned* — real earnings beats, not rotation luck — and the errors that cost the give-back have each been turned into a permanent rule. The final seven weeks are not about catching up anymore. They are about whether sharper entries and disciplined risk can finally convert this strategy's bursts of edge into a lead it keeps.
